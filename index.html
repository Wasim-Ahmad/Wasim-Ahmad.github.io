About

I hold a PhD in Computer Science from Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics. My research sits at the point where unmanned aerial vehicles meet wireless communication: how a radio link behaves when one end of it is flying, and how far that link can be pushed in terrain where ground infrastructure is thin or absent.

Much of my work has gone into channel modelling for agricultural, maritime, mountainous and dense urban settings, and into the Internet of UAV Things, where collaborative cloud computing carries the load that an aircraft cannot. Alongside that I apply machine and deep learning to the networks themselves — meta-learning for path loss prediction, deep learning for intrusion detection on UAV links, and more recently the integration of large language models into intent-based vehicular and smart-city communication.

I am looking for postdoctoral positions and research collaboration, and I am interested in supervising students, writing competitive grant proposals, and producing work that holds up under scrutiny.

Research areas

UAV channel modelling

Air-to-ground propagation and path loss across agriculture, maritime, mountainous and urban environments, validated against measured link behaviour.

Internet of UAV Things

Collaborative cloud computing for heterogeneous UAV networks, including coverage and handover optimisation using harmony-search methods.

Learning for wireless networks

Meta-learning applied to path loss prediction, letting a model generalise to a new channel from very few measurements.

LLM-integrated communication

Intent-based vehicle-to-everything systems where a language model turns high-level intent into network policy, under latency and energy constraints.

Network security

Deep learning for intrusion detection on UAV links, and privacy-preserving IoT frameworks built on trigger-action platforms.

Geospatial machine learning

Landslide susceptibility mapping and terrain risk assessment, applying ML to environmental data in mountainous regions.
